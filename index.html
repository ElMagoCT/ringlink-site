Every ring is the whole system. The first one you switch on makes a Wi-Fi network for your phone. The others find it over ESP-NOW and pass every message along, gate to gate, so a whole course follows one tap.

Features

Built for the field,
not the bench.

Race mode

Every gate waits in blue. Tap START and gate 1 turns green. Fly through, tap NEXT, and the green moves to the next gate. Laps, a 3-2-1 countdown, split times, undo, and a rainbow finish.

No router needed

Rings talk to each other directly. The first one on hosts the LED-Rings Wi-Fi and the page at rings.local. Switch the host off and another takes over in about five seconds.

Up to 50 rings

Every ring relays what it hears, up to seven hops, so a line of gates about 50 ft apart stays in sync. The app shows each ring's signal.

22 effects, five that run down the course

Course Wave, Relay, Ripple, Alternate and Course Rainbow follow your gate order, so the light travels the track the way the pilot does.

Course Wave

One 18650, USB-C

An IP5310 charges the cell and boosts it to 5 V. The app shows every ring's battery. A low ring blinks red, then switches its LEDs off before the cell runs flat.

Measures its own strip

On its first battery boot a ring lights its LEDs in steps, watches the voltage dip and works out how many LEDs it has. No config file.

Update every ring at once

Upload one file and the host updates every ring it can reach, over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. A bad update rolls itself back.

Tripod mount

A 1/4-20 T-nut in the base fits any camera tripod or light stand.

Five sizes

8, 12, 16, 18 and 24 in. Rings from 16 in up carry a bent steel hoop inside so they stay round.

How it works

Three steps from
switch to start line.

  1. 01

    Switch on the rings

    The first ring on becomes the host. Each ring fills up with its battery level, then shows its firmware version in dots.

  2. 02

    Join “LED-Rings”

    An open Wi-Fi network with no internet. The control page pops up by itself at rings.local.

  3. 03

    Type the PIN and play

    You set the PIN when you flash. Every ring learns it, and each phone stays unlocked for 30 days.

Out of Wi-Fi range, or on a laptop? Control over Bluetooth or USB opens the same controls from the flasher site. On iPhone, use the free Bluefy browser for Bluetooth.

Build guide

Print, wire, snap together.

Tools: a soldering iron, a multimeter, flush cutters, heat shrink and a 1/4-20 bolt to pull in the T-nut. Photos of each step are on the way.

A

Print

Print in clear TPU, one part per plate, laid out like this:

  • Sections: front face down.
  • Base: standing on its bottom.
  • Cover: outer face down.

No supports and no brim. Dry the filament for 6–8 h at 50–60 °C first; wet TPU strings and turns cloudy.

Your printer needs a bed of at least 241 mm for the size you picked.

C

Assemble

  1. Get the strip ready

    Cut one piece with 71 LEDs, cutting across the copper pads. Solder three leads to the start pads before you thread it.

  2. Make the divider stick

    Solder the two 100k resistors end to end with a lead off the middle, then heat-shrink them into one stick about 4 × 18 mm.

  3. Solder the cell

    Tabbed 18650, short 20 AWG leads: + to the toggle's middle lug, − to IP5310 B−.

  4. Fit the toggle

    The toggle goes out the left side of the base. Its outer lug goes to IP5310 B+.

  5. Charger and ESP32

    Stack them at the cell end of the bay. Wire 5V OUT to the ESP32 and the strip leads, and GPIO10 through the 330 Ω to DIN.

  6. Fit the divider stick

    Top lead to IP5310 B+ on the toggle side, middle lead to GPIO3, bottom lead to G.

  7. Flash the firmware

    Ring switch off, ESP32 on USB, then open the web flasher in Chrome or Edge and choose your PIN. Do this before the cover goes on; after that, updates go over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

  8. Thread the strip

    Feed it through the tunnel. Its leads come through the wire slot into the bay. Then bend the steel hoop to the curve and thread it through the lobe; its ends meet in the base.

  9. Pin the joints

    Push the parts together and lock each joint with a short piece of 1.75 mm filament. 4 pins for this size.

  10. Snap on the cover

    It closes the bay over the electronics.

  11. Press in the T-nut

    Pull it home with a 1/4-20 bolt so a tripod screws straight in.

  12. Switch it on

    On battery, a new ring lights its strip white in steps, then the LEDs it found in green. Then it shows its battery and version. Calibrate the battery reading once: measure B+/B− with a meter, type the reading into the Rings tab and tap Calibrate.

Questions

Good to know.

Do I need internet or a router?

No. The rings make their own Wi-Fi network, LED-Rings, and talk to each other over ESP-NOW. Your phone joins that network and opens rings.local.

How many rings can I run?

The firmware handles up to 50 rings in one set and up to 50 gates in the course order. We've flown four together so far, so tell us how bigger sets go.

How far apart can the gates be?

Each ring passes messages on, up to seven hops, so a line of gates about 50 ft apart works. The Rings tab shows each ring's signal; aim for 90 % of messages heard.

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes. The Wi-Fi control page works in Safari, and you can add it to your home screen. For Bluetooth control on iPhone, use the free Bluefy browser.

How do I update rings once the cover is on?

From the app: Rings → Firmware → pick the update file → Update all rings. It works over Wi-Fi from the host, or over Bluetooth from the flasher site, and each ring rolls back by itself if an update fails.

Can I sell rings I build?

Not without asking. Everything here is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: free to build, share and change for non-commercial use, with credit, and changes shared under the same terms.

Why clear TPU?

It flexes instead of cracking when a quad clips it, and the clear plastic spreads the LED light through the whole ring instead of showing dots.
