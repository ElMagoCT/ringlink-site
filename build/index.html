Build guide

Build a Crystal Gate.

Print the parts, wire five small boards, snap it together and flash it from your browser. These steps are for the Standard ring; pick your size and the numbers below follow.

0

What you need

  • Printed parts in clear TPU: 4 ring parts and a cover
  • 18650 cell, tabbed (never solder to the can)
  • IP5310 USB-C charger + 5 V boost board
  • ESP32-C3 SuperMini
  • WS2812B strip, 60 LEDs/m, IP30, 10 mm wide: 71 LEDs
  • MTS-102 mini toggle switch
  • Resistors: 2 × 100 kΩ (battery sense) and 1 × 330 Ω (LED data)
  • 1/4"-20 T-nut for the tripod mount
  • 9 ga steel wire for the hoop: 1.16 m
  • 22 AWG silicone wire (20 AWG for the battery leads) and heat shrink
  • 1.75 mm filament offcuts to pin the joints
  • Tools: soldering iron, multimeter, flush cutters, a 1/4-20 bolt

The full parts list with links and the best prices, and the STL files, are on MakerWorld.

A

Print

Print in clear TPU, one part per plate, laid out like this:

  • Sections: front face down.
  • Base: standing on its bottom.
  • Cover: outer face down.

No supports and no brim. Dry the filament for 6–8 h at 50–60 °C first; wet TPU strings and turns cloudy.

Your printer needs a bed of at least 241 mm for the size you picked.

C

Assemble

  1. Get the strip ready

    Cut one piece with 71 LEDs, cutting across the copper pads. Solder three leads to the start pads before you thread it.

  2. Make the divider stick

    Solder the two 100k resistors end to end with a lead off the middle, then heat-shrink them into one stick about 4 × 18 mm.

  3. Solder the cell

    Tabbed 18650, short 20 AWG leads: + to the toggle's middle lug, − to IP5310 B−.

  4. Fit the toggle

    The toggle goes out the left side of the base. Its outer lug goes to IP5310 B+.

  5. Charger and ESP32

    Stack them at the cell end of the bay. Wire 5V OUT to the ESP32 and the strip leads, and GPIO10 through the 330 Ω to DIN.

  6. Fit the divider stick

    Top lead to IP5310 B+ on the toggle side, middle lead to GPIO3, bottom lead to G.

  7. Flash the firmware

    Ring switch off, ESP32 on USB, then open the web flasher in Chrome or Edge and choose your PIN. Do this before the cover goes on; after that, updates go over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.

  8. Thread the strip

    Feed it through the tunnel. Its leads come through the wire slot into the bay. Then bend the steel hoop to the curve and thread it through the lobe; its ends meet in the base.

  9. Pin the joints

    Push the parts together and lock each joint with a short piece of 1.75 mm filament. 4 pins for this size.

  10. Snap on the cover

    It closes the bay over the electronics.

  11. Press in the T-nut

    Pull it home with a 1/4-20 bolt so a tripod screws straight in.

  12. Switch it on

    On battery, a new ring lights its strip white in steps, then the LEDs it found in green. Then it shows its battery and version. Calibrate the battery reading once: measure B+/B− with a meter, type the reading into the Rings tab and tap Calibrate.

D

Sense version

The Sense ring is the Standard ring plus a TEMT6000 light sensor at 6 o'clock under the inner face, behind a 0.4 mm slit in a two-part shroud. Print the shroud in black PLA or PETG so only the slit lets light in. Measure your sensor module before printing; its size is assumed in the CAD. Lap counting in the firmware is still to come.
